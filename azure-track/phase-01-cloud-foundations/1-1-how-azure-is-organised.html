1.1 How Azure is organised

Before any service, learn the containers everything lives in. Most Azure confusion comes from not knowing which level you are working at.

TL;DR
Azure is a hierarchy: a Microsoft Entra tenant holds identities, management groups organise subscriptions, a subscription is the billing and access boundary, a resource group holds related resources, and the resources sit inside. Policy and access set high in the tree flow down. Resources live in a region, and some regions offer availability zones.

The hierarchy

Access and policy inherit down this tree. Delete a resource group and everything inside goes with it.

Each level in practice

A tenant is an instance of Microsoft Entra ID. It is where users, groups and applications are defined. One organisation usually has one tenant, with many subscriptions attached to it.

Containers above subscriptions. Assign a policy or role on a management group and it applies to every subscription beneath it. This is how organisations enforce rules at scale, and it is the basis of landing zones (9.3).

The unit of billing and a boundary for access and quotas. Common patterns separate production from non-production, or one subscription per team or workload. A subscription belongs to exactly one tenant at a time.

Every resource lives in exactly one resource group. Group by lifecycle: things deployed and deleted together. Deleting a resource group deletes its resources, which makes it handy for tearing down a practice project.

Regions and availability zones

A region is a geographic area with datacentres, such as West Europe or Central India. Choose regions for latency, data residency rules, service availability and price. Within many regions, availability zones are physically separate locations with independent power, cooling and networking. Spreading a workload across zones protects against a single datacentre failure. Not every region has zones and not every service supports them, so check before you design. Many regions are also paired for disaster recovery planning.

Where does it belong?

You want one policy, 'allowed regions only', to apply to every subscription in the company.
Management groups exist to apply policy and access across many subscriptions with one assignment.
You are practising and want to delete everything from a tutorial in one command.
Resource groups share a lifecycle, so deleting the group removes its resources.
You need your app to survive the loss of a single datacentre in a region.
Zones are separate physical locations within a region, giving datacentre level resilience.

Check your understanding

1. What is the billing and quota boundary in Azure?
Billing, access and many limits are scoped to the subscription.
2. What happens when you delete a resource group?
Resources in the group are deleted with it, which is a useful cleanup tool and a risk.
3. What is an availability zone?
Zones give resilience against single datacentre failures.
