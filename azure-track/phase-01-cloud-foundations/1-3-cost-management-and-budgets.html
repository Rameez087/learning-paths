1.3 Cost management and budgets

The cloud bills by usage, and surprises happen to everyone. Set guardrails before your first experiment, not after your first bill.

TL;DR
Use cost analysis to see spend, budgets with alerts to get warned early, tags to attribute cost, and Azure Advisor for savings suggestions. Budgets alert, they do not stop resources by default. Put a budget on every subscription you practise in, and tear down projects when done.

Your guardrails

Set these up before building

Do this before the first project in this track.

Tools

Break spend down by service, resource group, tag or location. Look at daily trends to spot a resource that was left running.

Set an amount and alert thresholds. A budget notifies people or triggers an action group. It does not stop spending on its own unless you build automation for that.

Name and value pairs on resources. They make cost reports attributable, for example by project or owner. Enforce required tags with Azure Policy (5.3).

Azure Advisor recommends right sizing, removing idle resources and buying reservations. Treat recommendations as input, not orders.

Services that surprise beginners
Resources that keep billing when you are not using them include running VMs, provisioned databases, firewalls, VPN gateways, premium tier services and public IP addresses. Serverless and consumption options usually bill per use. Always check the pricing page of a service and the pricing calculator before you create it.

Why is the bill high?

You finished a tutorial a week ago and the bill keeps growing.
Idle but provisioned resources keep billing. Deleting the resource group is the clean fix.
You set a budget of 50 and spend reaches 120. Why was nothing stopped?
A budget is a monitoring tool. Automation or manual action must stop spending.

Check your understanding

1. What does a budget do by default?
Budgets notify. They do not enforce limits on their own.
2. Why use tags for cost management?
Tags make cost reports meaningful.
3. What is the simplest way to clean up a practice project?
One resource group per project makes teardown a single command.
