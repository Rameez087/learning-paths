7.1 Lake design and the medallion pattern

A data lake without structure becomes a swamp. A few conventions keep it usable.

TL;DR
Organise a lake into layers of increasing quality. The common medallion pattern uses bronze (raw, as received), silver (cleaned, conformed, deduplicated) and gold (business ready, modelled). Store in open table formats such as Delta Lake for transactions and schema enforcement. Separate storage accounts or containers by layer and environment, and apply ACLs and RBAC per zone.
Each hop adds quality and removes surprises for consumers.
LayerContentsRules
BronzeRaw data exactly as ingested, with load metadataAppend only, keep history, no business logic
SilverTyped, deduplicated, validated, conformed keysEnforce schema, quarantine bad rows
GoldAggregates and dimensional models for specific usesNamed and documented for consumers

Folder and naming conventions

abfss://bronze@lakeprod.dfs.core.windows.net/sales/orders/ingest_date=2026-10-11/
abfss://silver@lakeprod.dfs.core.windows.net/sales/orders/
abfss://gold@lakeprod.dfs.core.windows.net/sales/fact_orders/
  • Partition by a column that matches how data is filtered, usually a date, and avoid millions of tiny files.
  • Keep raw data immutable so you can always rebuild downstream layers.
  • Use Delta or similar for ACID writes, time travel and merges instead of bare Parquet when you update data.
  • Record lineage and ownership for every dataset (7.6).
Medallion is a pattern, not a law
Some teams use more layers or different names. The point is staged quality with clear contracts between stages.
