9.2 Reliability and the Well-Architected Framework

Good architecture is a set of trade offs made on purpose. The Well-Architected Framework gives you the checklist.

TL;DR
The Azure Well-Architected Framework has five pillars: Reliability, Security, Cost Optimization, Operational Excellence, Performance Efficiency. For reliability, define SLOs, design for failure with zones and regions, set RTO (how fast to recover) and RPO (how much data you may lose), and test recovery.
Pillars pull against each other: more resilience costs more.
TermMeaningDrives
SLOTarget level, such as 99.9 percent availabilityArchitecture choices
RTOMaximum acceptable downtimeStandby strategy (cold, warm, hot)
RPOMaximum acceptable data lossReplication method

Availability maths: composite availability is the product of dependencies in series. Two services at 99.9 percent in series give about 99.8 percent. Redundant paths in parallel raise it. Always check each service's published SLA and its conditions.

A backup you never restored is a hope
Schedule restore tests and failover drills. Document results and fix what they reveal.

Match the need

Losing five minutes of data is acceptable but a day is not.
RPO sets how much loss you can tolerate.
The app must stay up if a datacentre fails.
Zones isolate datacentre failures.
