9.3 Landing zones and governance at scale

A landing zone is a prepared environment: network, identity, policy and subscriptions ready before workloads arrive.

TL;DR
An Azure landing zone is a multi subscription environment built on the Cloud Adoption Framework. A management group hierarchy separates platform subscriptions (identity, connectivity, management) from application subscriptions. Policy and RBAC are assigned at management groups, a hub network provides connectivity, and subscriptions are created on demand (subscription vending) so teams get a ready, compliant home.
Policy flows down. Platform teams run the middle, application teams own their subscriptions.
  • Deploy it with code (Bicep or Terraform, using the landing zone accelerators and AVM).
  • Assign a policy initiative for guardrails: allowed regions, required tags, no public storage, diagnostics on.
  • Centralise logs in a workspace owned by the platform.
  • Automate subscription vending so a new team gets a subscription, network peering, RBAC and budgets from a request.
Scale to need
A five person startup does not need the full enterprise reference architecture. Start with a small subset: a management group, a policy baseline and separate production and non production subscriptions, then grow.

What belongs where?

A shared firewall and VPN gateway.
Shared services live in the hub.
A team wants to try a service freely, with no connection to company data.
Sandboxes isolate experiments.
