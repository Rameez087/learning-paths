10.2 The fix: circuit breakers, rate limiting, graceful degradation

Three complementary defenses: stop calling what's failing, protect yourself from being overwhelmed, and keep working with less rather than not working at all.

"Added circuit breakers on every inter-service call. Ran the exact same Notifications slowdown scenario in staging last week — this time, Feed detected the failures after a few seconds, stopped calling Notifications entirely, served feeds without the 'new notification' badge, and stayed completely healthy. No cascade." — you, after the fix shipped
TL;DR
A circuit breaker wraps calls to a dependency and tracks recent failures — after enough failures in a short window, it "trips open" and stops attempting calls entirely for a cooldown period, failing fast instead of waiting on a slow dependency and directly preventing 10.1's resource exhaustion. Rate limiting protects a service from being overwhelmed by more requests than it can handle, from any source, including well-meaning internal callers during a retry storm. Graceful degradation means designing features to keep working, just with reduced functionality, when a dependency is unavailable — Feed still loads without notification badges, rather than failing entirely.

Try it: watch a circuit breaker trip and recover

The circuit breaker's three states, and why the middle one matters most

Closed (normal): calls pass through freely, failures are tracked. Open (tripped): calls fail immediately without even attempting the network call, giving the struggling dependency room to recover instead of continuing to hammer it. Half-open (testing recovery): after a cooldown, a small number of test calls are allowed through — if they succeed, the circuit closes again; if they fail, it reopens. This half-open state is what makes the whole mechanism self-healing rather than needing a human to manually decide when it's safe to resume.

Rate limiting: protecting yourself, not just protecting others

Chapter 7.3's idempotency dealt with duplicate deliveries; this is different — rate limiting caps how many requests a service accepts in a given window, rejecting the excess outright rather than trying to process everything and collapsing under the volume. Critically, this applies even to well-meaning internal traffic — a retry storm from many circuit breakers all reopening and testing the same recovering service at once can itself overwhelm that service all over again without a rate limit in place.

Graceful degradation: deciding in advance what's essential

The core design question: for any given feature, what's the minimum viable version if a specific dependency is unavailable? Feed loading without notification badges is a reasonable degraded experience — the feed itself, the actually essential part, still works. This requires deciding, ahead of an actual incident, which parts of a feature are genuinely essential and which are enhancements that can be silently dropped under stress.

1. How does a circuit breaker directly prevent 10.1's resource exhaustion problem?
This is the direct mechanism — failing fast instead of waiting is exactly what prevents the resource-holding pattern that caused 10.1's cascade in the first place.
2. Why is the circuit breaker's half-open state important for the mechanism to be self-healing?
This automatic recovery testing is what distinguishes a genuinely self-healing system from one that requires manual intervention every time a dependency recovers.
3. Why does rate limiting need to apply even to well-meaning internal traffic, not just external abuse?
This is a genuinely important, non-obvious detail — the very mechanism designed to help (circuit breakers testing recovery) can itself become a source of overwhelming load without a rate limit as a backstop.
