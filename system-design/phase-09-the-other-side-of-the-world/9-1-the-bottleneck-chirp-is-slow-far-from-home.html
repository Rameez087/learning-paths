9.1 The bottleneck: Chirp is slow far from home

Every single piece of Chirp's infrastructure — every service from Chapter 8, every shard from Chapter 6 — lives in one US data center. Chirp just started trending in Southeast Asia.

⚠ Alert
Median latency for users in the US sits at a healthy 90ms. Median latency for new users in Singapore, Jakarta, and Manila is running 480-650ms — even though every earlier fix in this track is working exactly as designed.
"Nothing is actually broken. Every service, every cache, every shard is healthy and fast. The problem is physics — a request from Manila to our data center in Virginia and back covers roughly 28,000 kilometers round trip, and that simply takes time no amount of clever engineering removes." — you, in the incident channel
TL;DR
Every fix through Chapter 8 assumed users were roughly the same network distance from Chirp's infrastructure — true when Chirp was primarily a US product, false the moment real growth starts coming from other continents. Network latency — the time for data to physically travel between two points — has a genuine floor set by the speed of light, and no amount of caching, sharding, or service optimization changes the actual distance data has to travel. This is the first bottleneck in this entire track that isn't really about capacity, contention, or coordination at all — it's pure physical distance.

Why this floor is genuinely unavoidable, not just "not yet optimized"

Light (and the electrical/optical signals data actually travels as) moves at a fixed maximum speed. A request from Manila to a data center in Virginia has a physical minimum round-trip time no engineering effort can beat — real-world fiber routing, through undersea cables and intermediate hops, typically adds meaningfully more than the theoretical minimum on top of that. This is worth stating plainly: unlike every earlier bottleneck in this track, this one has a hard physical floor that no clever architecture eliminates, only works around.

Why "just add more servers in Virginia" doesn't help at all here

Every previous fix in this track added capacity or split work to solve a bottleneck rooted in how much work needed doing. This bottleneck isn't about volume at all — a Manila user making one single request still has to travel the same physical distance, no matter how much idle capacity Virginia has. The only way to genuinely reduce this latency is to reduce the physical distance the request has to travel — which means putting infrastructure physically closer to where users actually are.
