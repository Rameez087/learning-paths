9.2 The fix: CDN edge & multi-region

Can't beat the speed of light — so put a real copy of Chirp physically closer to where the speed of light needs to travel less far.

"Stood up a full regional deployment in Singapore — app servers, a database region, the works — plus expanded our CDN's edge presence for media. Southeast Asia median latency dropped from 560ms to 95ms. Basically the whole distance problem, gone." — you, after the rollout
TL;DR
Two complementary fixes, operating at different layers. Expanding Chapter 4's CDN to more edge locations worldwide handles static content (images, video, CSS) — genuinely simple, since that content is identical for everyone regardless of region. Multi-region deployment goes further: standing up complete, independent copies of Chirp's actual application infrastructure (app servers, and eventually databases) in multiple geographic regions, so a Singapore user's dynamic requests — not just their images — get served from nearby infrastructure instead of traveling to Virginia every time.

Try it: route users to their nearest region

Why static content was the easy part

A CDN caching a profile picture is straightforward precisely because that image is identical regardless of who requests it or from where — there's no personalization, no per-user logic, nothing that needs to check a database. Expanding CDN edge coverage to more of the world was genuinely the simple half of this fix, largely an extension of Chapter 4's existing pattern to more locations.

Why dynamic, personalized requests are the genuinely hard half

A feed request isn't identical for everyone — it depends on who's asking, who they follow, and data that has to actually be looked up. Serving that from a nearby region means that region needs its own copy of the relevant application logic and its own access to the relevant data — which raises a real question this page is about to leave open on purpose: does each region get its own independent database, or do all regions somehow share one? Either answer has real consequences, and the next page picks up exactly this tension.

DNS-based routing: how a user actually reaches their nearest region

A specific mechanism worth naming: geo-aware DNS resolves chirp.com to a different IP address depending on where the request is coming from — a Singapore user's DNS lookup returns the Singapore region's address, a Virginia user's returns Virginia's. This happens transparently before the user's request even leaves their device, routing them to nearby infrastructure automatically.
