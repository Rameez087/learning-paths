11.10 Leaderless replication & quorums

Chirp always had exactly one writable copy per shard. Here's the genuinely different model Cassandra and DynamoDB use instead — no leader at all.

TL;DR
In leaderless replication, any replica can accept a write — there's no single primary to route through, unlike every design in Chirp's story. Correctness comes from quorums: with N total replicas, a write must succeed on at least W of them, and a read must check at least R of them. As long as W + R > N, every read is guaranteed to overlap with the most recent write on at least one replica — the same overlap guarantee from 11.6's quorum discussion, now applied specifically to guaranteeing read freshness rather than just failure tolerance.

Why Chirp's actual design (single-leader) was still the right call

Leaderless replication isn't strictly better than Chapter 5's single-leader model — it's a different trade-off. Single-leader gives a simple, clear ordering of writes and avoids 5.3-style conflicts entirely, at the cost of the primary being a bottleneck for writes. Leaderless spreads write load across every replica with no single bottleneck, at the cost of needing to actively resolve concurrent writes to the same key — a real, recurring cost, not a one-time setup decision.

What happens when quorums don't overlap: stale reads, by design

Choosing W + R ≤ N is a legitimate, deliberate choice some systems make — it trades away the freshness guarantee for even lower latency (fewer replicas need to respond per operation). This is the same latency-vs-consistency trade-off from 11.3's PACELC framework, made explicit and tunable per-operation rather than baked into the architecture once, the way Chirp's single-leader design baked it in back in Chapter 5.2.

Read repair: fixing staleness quietly, after the fact

When a quorum read discovers that different replicas disagree (one has an older value than another), the coordinating node can write the newer value back to the stale replica right then — read repair. This means staleness in a leaderless system tends to self-correct over time as reads happen, rather than requiring a separate, dedicated reconciliation process.
