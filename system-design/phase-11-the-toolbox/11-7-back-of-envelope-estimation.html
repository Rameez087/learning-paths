11.7 Back-of-envelope estimation

Every chapter in this track had specific numbers — 340 queries/sec, 91% cache hit rate, 8 shards. Here's how those numbers actually get estimated, before a system exists to measure.

TL;DR
Before designing anything, a rough estimate of scale — requests per second, storage growth, bandwidth — reveals which bottlenecks (from this entire track) are actually going to matter for a specific system, versus which are irrelevant at its actual scale. The method: start from a stated or reasonable assumption (total users, daily active fraction), derive requests per second, then derive storage and bandwidth from there — accepting round numbers and orders of magnitude over false precision.

Try it: estimate Chirp's own numbers at a given scale

The core estimation chain, step by step

# Step 1: daily active users (DAU) — commonly ~20-30% of total registered
total_users = 10_000_000
dau = total_users * 0.25  # 2.5M

# Step 2: average actions per user per day
avg_feed_refreshes_per_user = 15
total_daily_requests = dau * avg_feed_refreshes_per_user  # 37.5M/day

# Step 3: convert to requests per second (accounting for uneven traffic)
avg_qps = total_daily_requests / 86_400  # ~434 QPS average
peak_qps = avg_qps * 3  # peak traffic is often 2-3x average — ~1,300 QPS

# Step 4: storage — one post, roughly estimated
avg_post_size_bytes = 300  # text + metadata, excluding media
posts_per_day = dau * 0.3  # 30% of DAU post at least once
daily_storage = posts_per_day * avg_post_size_bytes  # ~225 MB/day of text alone

Why round numbers and orders of magnitude are the actual goal

The point of this exercise isn't computing an exact figure — it's determining whether a system needs to handle thousands, millions, or billions of requests, since the right architecture genuinely differs by orders of magnitude (recall how differently this track's every chapter's fix was shaped by roughly what scale Chirp had actually reached). Getting the exact number right to three significant figures adds essentially no value; getting the right order of magnitude is what actually determines whether Chapter 3's load balancing or Chapter 6's sharding is the relevant concern.

Why this matters specifically in an interview setting

Recall Design Gurus' framing from Chapter 1.1: interviewers grade whether a candidate can reason from constraints to a defensible design. Stating "let's assume 10 million daily active users, which is roughly 400 average QPS with peaks around 1,200" — and then designing around those numbers — demonstrates exactly that reasoning, visibly, rather than jumping straight to architecture without any grounding in actual scale.
