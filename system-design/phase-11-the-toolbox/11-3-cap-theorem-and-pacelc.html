11.3 CAP theorem & PACELC

Chapters 5.3 and 9.3 both lived through this theorem without naming it. Here's the formal vocabulary for what you already have real intuition for.

TL;DR
CAP theorem: in the presence of a network partition, a distributed system must choose between Consistency (every read sees the latest write) and Availability (every request gets a response, even if not the latest data) — you cannot have both during a partition. Partition tolerance isn't really a choice for any real distributed system — networks fail, so it's a given, and CAP is really about the C-vs-A choice specifically during those failures. PACELC extends this usefully: even when there's no partition, a system still trades Latency for Consistency — which is exactly the choice Chapter 5.2 made when choosing asynchronous replication.

Try it: place real Chirp decisions on the CAP/PACELC map

Why "partition tolerance isn't optional" is the key insight most summaries skip

A common oversimplification treats CAP as "pick 2 of 3." In practice, real distributed systems span multiple machines (and, since Chapter 9, multiple continents) connected by real networks — and real networks partition sometimes, whether anyone wants them to or not. A system doesn't get to opt out of network failures, so the actual, meaningful decision is what happens when a partition occurs: does the system stay available (like Chirp choosing to serve slightly stale replica reads in 5.2/5.3) or does it refuse to answer until consistency can be guaranteed again.

PACELC's genuinely useful addition

CAP alone only describes behavior during a partition — but partitions are relatively rare; most of the time, the system is running normally. PACELC fills this gap: if Partitioned, choose Availability or Consistency (this is CAP); Else (normal operation), choose Latency or Consistency. This second half is exactly what Chapter 5.2's asynchronous replication decision was — no partition involved at all, just a deliberate choice to accept eventual consistency in exchange for faster writes, made during completely normal operation.

Connecting this back to Chirp's actual decisions

Chapter 5.2 (asynchronous replication) is a PACELC "Latency over Consistency" choice, made during normal operation. Chapter 9.3's multi-region conflict resolution is fundamentally the same category of decision, just at larger scale. Neither chapter used this vocabulary at the time — the whole point of building this intuition through the story first was so the formal theorem now describes something already understood, rather than being an abstract rule memorized without context.
