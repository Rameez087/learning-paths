5.2 LLMs for data management

Schema matching, entity resolution, cleaning and text to SQL are classic data engineering problems that language models now attack. Where they fail is the research.

TL;DR
Foundation models can be prompted to do data wrangling tasks, often with few or no labelled examples. Narayan and colleagues showed this for entity matching, error detection and imputation. The open questions are where they fail (scale, cost, hallucination, domain specific schemas) and how to evaluate them on enterprise style data.

Tasks that now have LLM approaches

TaskWhat it isTypical failure to study
Entity resolutionDecide whether two records are the same real world entityCost per pair at scale; inconsistent answers across runs
Schema matchingAlign columns across two schemasCryptic column names such as SAP style codes
Data cleaning and error detectionFind and fix wrong or inconsistent valuesConfident but wrong fixes with no way to verify
ImputationFill missing valuesPlausible but invented values
Text to SQLTranslate a question into a queryWrong joins on large, ambiguously named schemas
The enterprise gap
Many benchmarks use clean, well named public tables. Enterprise systems have thousands of tables, opaque names, local conventions and strict correctness needs. Studying how methods behave on messy, large schemas is a gap you are unusually well placed to examine, using public or synthetic stand-ins so you never expose employer data.

Design a small study

1. Choose a task

Pick one: for example entity matching between two product catalogs from a public benchmark.

2. Choose a baseline

Start with string similarity and a simple classifier. The LLM has to beat this to matter.

3. Vary the condition

Add one stress factor at a time: abbreviated names, extra columns, larger candidate sets.

4. Measure cost and consistency

Report accuracy, cost per decision and agreement across repeated runs, not only accuracy.

5. Report failures

Classify the errors. A taxonomy of failures is often the most useful part of the paper.

Pick the stronger research question

Which is better scoped?
The first names a task, a stress factor, a baseline and a measurable degradation.

Check your understanding

1. Which gap is a data engineer well placed to study?
Enterprise style data is underrepresented in clean public benchmarks.
2. Why report cost and consistency, not just accuracy?
Practicality in pipelines depends on cost and repeatability.
3. What should an LLM method be compared against first?
A simple baseline shows whether the LLM adds anything.
