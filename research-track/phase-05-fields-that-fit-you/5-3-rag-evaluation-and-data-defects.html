5.3 RAG evaluation and data defects

Retrieval augmented generation works only as well as the documents it retrieves. Measuring how data defects change its quality is an open, practical question.

TL;DR
A RAG system retrieves documents then generates an answer. Errors can enter at either stage, and data defects upstream (duplicates, stale documents, missing fields, schema drift, bad chunking) change retrieval and answer quality. Evaluate retrieval with recall at k, MRR and nDCG, and generation with faithfulness and relevance, using tools such as Ragas.

Where defects enter a RAG pipeline

Defects introduced early (before the index) pass silently through every later stage.
DefectLikely effect to measure
Duplicate documentsRetrieved slots wasted on copies, lowering recall at k
Stale or conflicting versionsAnswers cite out of date or contradictory facts
Missing fields or metadataFilters and citations fail
Bad chunkingAnswers split across chunks, hurting retrieval and faithfulness
OCR or encoding noiseEmbeddings degrade, keywords fail
Schema driftParsing changes silently drop content

Metrics

What to measure

Recall at k: did the relevant document appear in the top k? MRR: how high was the first relevant result? nDCG: ranking quality with graded relevance.

Ragas defines metrics such as faithfulness (is the answer supported by the retrieved context) and answer relevance, and context precision and recall. Understand how each is computed and its limits, especially when a language model is the judge.

Sample and label a subset by hand. Automatic metrics can disagree with people, and a small human audit tells you how far to trust them.

Design a defect injection benchmark

1. Pick a public corpus and question set

Choose a dataset with questions and known relevant documents, so retrieval has ground truth. Check its licence.

2. Fix the pipeline

Hold the retriever, embedding model, chunk size and generator constant. Only the data will change.

3. Inject one defect at a time

For example add duplicates at 0, 10, 20 and 40 percent. Keep a clean copy as the control.

4. Measure with seeds

Report retrieval and generation metrics over several seeds, with intervals (3.2).

5. Analyse

Which defect hurts most, at what rate, and can a simple cleaning step recover the loss? That recovery curve is your headline result.

Which metric fits?

You inject duplicate documents and want to see whether relevant sources still reach the top 5 results.
Recall at k directly measures whether relevant documents are retrieved in the top k slots.
You want to know whether answers are supported by the retrieved passages.
Faithfulness checks grounding of the answer in the retrieved context.

Check your understanding

1. Why can upstream data defects be hard to notice?
Quality errors rarely raise exceptions, so you must measure them.
2. In a defect injection study, what stays fixed?
Holding the pipeline constant isolates the effect of the data change.
3. What is a good headline result for such a study?
Degradation and recovery curves tell practitioners what matters.

Sources: Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (Lewis et al.); Ragas: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation; Data Collection and Quality Challenges in Deep Learning (Whang et al.)
