8.4 Open communities and independent paths

Professors are one route into research, not the only one. Open communities can start the clock on collaboration and references now.

TL;DR
Open research communities such as Cohere Labs Community and EleutherAI welcome independent contributors, run projects in public and need no professor. They give you collaborators, feedback and a track record. Details change, so check each community's current entry process before counting on it.

Two communities

CommunityWhat its pages sayHow to approach
Cohere Labs CommunityAn open science community that collaborates with independent researchers worldwide. Membership is by application, and I did not verify that it is open nowRead its pages, check how to join, then propose or join a project from Phase 6
EleutherAIA grassroots, open research collectiveLook at open projects, especially evaluation and data work, and contribute small well-scoped pieces first

A 2025 study mapping open collaboration in open source AI covers both communities and may help you understand how they work.

Other independent routes

Fix bugs or add features in tools you use (evaluation libraries, lineage integrations). Maintainers become references, and your commits are public evidence.

Submit your Project A or B write-up to a workshop (1.3). Reviews are feedback even if rejected.

Some venues and courses run reproducibility challenges. They give structure and visibility to a reproduction. Check what is running now.

Public write-ups of your experiments, such as a clear blog post linked to the repo, attract replies from people in the field.

Do not wait
Open communities can start the clock now, in parallel with emails to professors. Start with a project from Phase 6 so you arrive with something to contribute.

Check your understanding

1. Why join an open community in parallel with emailing professors?
Parallel routes reduce dependence on any single reply.
2. What should you do before relying on a community's application process?
Programmes change, so confirm on their own pages.
3. Which is a good first contribution to a large open project?
Small useful contributions build trust.
