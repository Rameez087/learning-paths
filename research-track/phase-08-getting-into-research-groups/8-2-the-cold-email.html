8.2 The cold email

A short email that shows you read their work and asks one answerable question gets read. Most long, generic ones do not.

TL;DR
Keep it short, put the topic in the subject line, mention one or two specifics from their work, attach your CV, link your artifact, and ask one question they can answer in a line. Offer a small next step such as a 15 minute call. Follow up once after about a week. Write it in your own voice.

The rules the guides agree on

  • Keep it short, and say who you are and what you are asking for.
  • Put the topic in the subject line.
  • Mention one or two specifics from their work, so it is clear this is not a mass email.
  • Ask a question they can answer in a line, not an open request for mentorship.
  • Attach your CV, and link your repo once you have one.
  • If there is no reply after about a week, send one gentle follow up.
  • Write it in your own voice. An AI tool can help with structure, but the words should be yours.

Template to adapt

Subject: Question on [paper title]: data defects in RAG pipelines

Dear Professor [Surname],

I am a software engineer working on data pipelines for enterprise systems, and I am applying to [programme] for [intake]. I read your paper "[title]" and was interested in [one specific point, one sentence].

Over the last [N] weeks I reproduced [paper] and extended it with [one change]. The repo and a two page report are here: [link]. My question is whether [narrow question] is still open, or whether your group has already covered it.

Would you be open to a 15 minute call, or could you point me to someone in your group I should speak with? My CV is attached.

Thank you for your time,
[Your name]
Send only when the link works
Without a public artifact the email asks for a favour. With it, the email offers evidence. Build the repo first (Project A, 6.1).

Pick the better opening

Which opening is stronger?
The first shows you read the work and asks something specific. The second is flattery with no content.

Check your understanding

1. What is the best kind of question to ask in a first email?
Easy to answer means more likely to be answered.
2. When should you follow up if there is no reply?
A single polite follow up is standard. Silence is often just a busy inbox.
3. Why mention specifics from their work?
Specifics signal real interest and effort.
