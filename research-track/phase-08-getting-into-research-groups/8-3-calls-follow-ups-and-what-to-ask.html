8.3 Calls, follow-ups and what to ask

When someone replies, the real work begins. Prepare for a short call so you use their time well and leave a good impression.

TL;DR
Before a call, reread their recent work and prepare three precise questions. During it, listen more than you talk, ask what questions matter in the area and what a first project could look like. After it, send a short thank you within a day, with one concrete follow-up such as a sharpened plan.

The call, step by step

Before: prepare

Reread two recent papers. Write your one sentence project summary. Prepare three questions: one about their work, one about your project and one about how people join the group. Test your link and audio.

Open: be brief

Give a 60 second introduction: who you are, what you built, what you want to learn. Do not recite your CV.

Middle: ask and listen

Ask what open questions they find most interesting, whether your idea overlaps with something the group already did, and what a realistic first contribution looks like. Take notes.

Close: a next step

Ask whether there is anyone else you should talk to, and agree on the next step, for example sending a short plan or a draft report.

After: follow up

Send a thank you within 24 hours. Summarise what you learned and what you will do. Then do it, and send the result.

What to avoid
Do not ask for a position in the first call. Do not ask for a favour before showing work. Do not criticise other groups. Do not leave without a next step.

Follow-up timeline

Check your understanding

1. What should the 60 second introduction cover?
Brevity respects their time and sets up the discussion.
2. When should you send a thank you?
Prompt, specific thanks shows professionalism.
3. Why end the call with an agreed next step?
A next step creates momentum and accountability.
