6.2 Project B: data defects in RAG

A benchmark that measures how upstream data problems damage a RAG system. Original enough to be interesting, small enough to finish.

TL;DR
Take a public question answering corpus, inject controlled defects (duplicates, stale documents, missing fields, schema drift) at several rates, hold the pipeline fixed and measure retrieval and answer quality. The artifact is a benchmark script, a results table and the degradation curves. Plan 8 to 12 weeks, after Project A.

Design

Project at a glance

How much does each type of data defect, at increasing rates, degrade retrieval recall and answer faithfulness for a fixed RAG pipeline, and how much can simple cleaning recover?

Public corpus with questions and known relevant documents. One fixed retriever, embedder, chunker and generator. Defect injectors written as small functions with a rate and a seed. Controls with zero defects.

Recall at k, MRR, faithfulness and answer relevance (Ragas), with seeds and intervals. Cost and runtime per condition.

Public repo, one command to rerun the benchmark, a results table, degradation curves and a short write-up.

Milestones

Check novelty first
Before you commit weeks, spend two days on Google Scholar and arXiv searching for your exact question with several phrasings. Related work likely exists on data quality in RAG, so decide how yours differs, for example the specific defect types or the recovery analysis.

Scope check

You want to test 6 defect types, 5 retrievers, 4 generators and 3 corpora. What should you do?
A narrow design that finishes beats a broad one that never does. Hold the pipeline fixed so the data effect is clear.

Check your understanding

1. Why hold the pipeline fixed while injecting defects?
If several things vary, you cannot attribute degradation to the defect.
2. What is the control condition?
The clean control provides the reference for measuring degradation.
3. What belongs in the artifact?
Reproducible code and results are what make it citable and checkable.

Sources: Ragas: Automated Evaluation of Retrieval Augmented Generation; Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks (Lewis et al.); Data Collection and Quality Challenges in Deep Learning (Whang et al.)
