6.1 Project A: reproduce and extend one paper

Your first project. It finishes on a deadline, teaches the whole loop and gives you a public artifact to point to in every email.

TL;DR
Choose one recent paper with released code or enough detail, reproduce its main table, record every gap between your numbers and theirs, then add one extension: an ablation, a new dataset or a stronger baseline. Publish the repo and a two page report. Plan 6 to 9 weeks.

Choose the paper

A good paper to reproduce

The nine week path

Weeks 1 to 2: read and plan

Do passes one and two on the paper (2.1). Write its claim table (1.2). Decide the exact table you will reproduce and write the environment plan: language version, libraries, data download.

Weeks 3 to 4: get it running

Run the authors' code on the smallest setup that works. Log seeds and config (4.3). Do not fix anything yet, just record what happens, including failures.

Weeks 5 to 6: match the numbers

Run the main experiment over several seeds (3.2). Compare your table with theirs. For every gap, note a hypothesis: different library version, unreported hyperparameter, different split. Test the likely ones.

Weeks 7 to 8: the extension

Add exactly one thing: an ablation they skipped, a second dataset, or a tougher baseline. State the claim and falsifier first (4.1).

Week 9: write and publish

Write the two page report (7.1). Clean the repo so it reproduces with one command. Publish it with a licence. Now you have the artifact for outreach (8.2).

What counts as success
A match is not required. A clear, documented explanation of why you did or did not match is the contribution. A rigorous report that the result is fragile is more useful than a lucky match.

Check your understanding

1. What is the minimum extension to add to a reproduction?
One focused extension keeps the project finishable and the result interpretable.
2. What if your numbers do not match the paper's?
Honest documentation of discrepancies is itself the contribution.
3. Why avoid papers that need private data?
Public data keeps the work checkable and safe to publish.
