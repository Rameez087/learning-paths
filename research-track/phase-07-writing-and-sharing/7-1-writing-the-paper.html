7.1 Writing the paper

Writing is not a final chore. It is how you discover whether you have a result. Draft the abstract and the main results figure first.

TL;DR
Write in this order: main results figure and table, abstract, method, experiments, introduction, related work, limitations. A good abstract has four moves: context, gap, what you did, what you found. A reader making a single pass should still get the whole message from the title, abstract, headings and figures.

The four move abstract

MoveQuestion it answersExample
ContextWhat area, and why does it matter?Retrieval augmented generation depends on the quality of its source documents.
GapWhat is unknown or broken?How specific data defects degrade retrieval is rarely measured.
What we didWhat did you do, concretely?We inject four defect types at controlled rates into a public corpus and measure retrieval and answer quality over five seeds.
What we foundWhat is the result, with a number?Duplicates at 20 percent lower recall at 5 by 4 points; simple deduplication recovers most of the loss. Code is released.

Try it: check your abstract

Paste a draft abstract. The checklist flags common problems. It is a crude set of rules, not a judge of quality, and nothing you type leaves your browser.

Order of writing

  1. Results figure and main table: the evidence
  2. Abstract: the claim in four sentences
  3. Method: enough to reimplement
  4. Experiments and ablations
  5. Introduction: problem, gap, contributions list
  6. Related work: where you sit among neighbours
  7. Limitations and conclusion: be honest

Which sentence is stronger?

Pick the better result sentence for an abstract.
Concrete numbers, an interval and a seed count let the reader check. The others are empty claims.

Check your understanding

1. Which should you write first?
The evidence determines what the rest of the paper can claim.
2. What are the four moves of an abstract?
This structure delivers the claim to a one pass reader.
3. Why avoid words like novel and groundbreaking?
Readers trust specific results, not adjectives.
