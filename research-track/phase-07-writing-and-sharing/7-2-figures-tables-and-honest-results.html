7.2 Figures, tables and honest results

A good figure lets a reader check your claim in seconds. A misleading one does the opposite, even when no one intends it.

TL;DR
Show spread, not just means. Label axes and units. Start bars at zero. Use the same scale when comparing panels. Report negative and mixed results. Make the main results table report mean and standard deviation over seeds, and bold only differences that are supported by your statistics.

Principles

DoAvoid
Error bars or intervals with the number of seeds statedSingle point estimates with no spread
Bars starting at zero, or clearly marked truncated axesTruncated axes that exaggerate small differences
One message per figure, with a title that states itOverloaded plots with eight series
Colour blind safe palettes and direct labelsRelying on colour alone
Bold only differences you can supportBolding the best number regardless of noise
Showing failure casesOnly showing cherry picked examples
The table template
Method, metric mean, standard deviation, number of seeds, and a note on the significance test. Put the baseline first. If your method is not clearly better, say so in the text. Honest mixed results are respected.

Spot the problem

A bar chart shows accuracy 78.1 vs 78.4, with the vertical axis running from 78.0 to 78.5.
Truncation exaggerates the gap, especially without error bars.
A table bolds the best score in every row even where differences are within seed noise.
Bold only differences backed by intervals or tests.
A figure shows only five hand picked good examples of your system.
Random samples and failures give an honest picture.

Check your understanding

1. What should a results table include beyond means?
Spread and seed count let readers judge significance.
2. Why is a truncated y axis risky?
Readers compare bar heights visually, so truncation misleads.
3. When should you bold a number in a table?
Bold implies a real difference, so back it with evidence.

Sources: NeurIPS Paper Checklist; Improving Reproducibility in ML Research (Pineau et al.)
