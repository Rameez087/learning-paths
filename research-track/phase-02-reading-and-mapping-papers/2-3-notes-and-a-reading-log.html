2.3 Notes and a reading log

Reading without notes evaporates in a week. A fixed template turns every paper into a reusable record and exposes what you have not understood.

TL;DR
Keep one log row per paper with the same columns every time: the question, the method, the data, the result, the limits and what you could reuse. Add a one paragraph summary in your own words. If you cannot write that paragraph, you have not finished pass two.

A template that works

FieldWhat to writeExample
CitationAuthors, year, venue, linkZha et al. 2023, arXiv, Data-centric AI
QuestionThe one question the paper answersHow should the field organise data-centric work?
MethodWhat they did, in two linesSurvey that groups tasks into three goals
DataDatasets, sizes, licencesNone (position paper)
ResultThe headline number or findingA taxonomy and open problems
LimitsWhat the authors admit and what you spottedBroad, few experiments
ReuseA figure, a dataset, a baseline or a citation you will useUse the three goal taxonomy to frame my project

Write the summary last and in your own words, in at most five sentences. Copying the abstract is a way of avoiding understanding. A useful test: could you explain the paper's evidence to a friend who works in data engineering without opening it?

Recall: what to capture

Check your understanding

1. Why write the paper summary in your own words rather than copying the abstract?
If you cannot rephrase the contribution, you probably need another pass.
2. Which log field most directly produces future research questions?
Limitations and open questions point at what has not been tested, which is where new work lives.
3. How should you organise a reference manager?
Project folders keep reading tied to a purpose and make writing related work easier.
