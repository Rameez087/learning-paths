4.1 Asking a falsifiable question

A good research question can be proven wrong. If no result could embarrass you, you have a topic, not a question.

TL;DR
Turn a topic into a claim with five parts: the method, the baseline, the metric, the dataset and the size of effect you expect. Then write the falsifier: the result that would show you wrong. Do this in two sentences before you run anything. It forces precision and protects you from moving the goalposts later.

From topic to claim

Which falsifier is stated correctly?
A falsifier is a pre-stated, measurable condition. Tuning until the claim works removes the possibility of being wrong.
Pre-registration, lightweight
Write the claim and falsifier in your repo's README and commit it before you run the main experiment. The timestamp shows you did not adjust the question after seeing the data. You can still explore, but label exploration as exploration.

Check your understanding

1. What makes a claim falsifiable?
If no outcome could contradict the claim, it is not testable.
2. When should you write the falsifier?
Stating it in advance prevents moving the goalposts.
3. Which part of a claim guards against a fluke?
Seeds and an interval turn a lucky single run into a testable statement.
