4.5 Compute budgets and cheap experiments

You do not need a cluster for a first paper. You need to design experiments small enough to repeat many times.

TL;DR
Plan your compute budget before you start: configurations times seeds times minutes per run. Shrink the problem until a full experiment takes hours, not weeks. Use subsets, smaller models and cached features. Many strong reproductions and benchmark papers run on a laptop or a free notebook service, but check each provider's current limits.

Try it: will your experiment fit?

Report your compute
The NeurIPS checklist asks authors to describe the compute used. Reporting your hardware and total hours is part of honest reproducibility, and it tells readers your result is within reach of others.

Check your understanding

1. Why plan compute before starting?
Repeating runs across seeds is essential, so total cost must be affordable.
2. What is a risk of subsampling data?
Subsampling is fine if you state it and test whether the effect holds across sizes.
3. What should you report about compute?
Compute reporting is part of the reproducibility checklist.

Sources: NeurIPS Paper Checklist; Improving Reproducibility in ML Research (Pineau et al.)
