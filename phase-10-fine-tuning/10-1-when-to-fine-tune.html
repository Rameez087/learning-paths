10.1 When to fine-tune vs. prompt or RAG

The most common fine-tuning mistake in 2026: reaching for it before the cheaper options have actually failed.

TL;DR
2026 practitioner guidance converges on a specific order: Prompt → RAG → Fine-tune → Distill — try the cheapest, fastest, fully reversible option first, and only escalate once it's demonstrably insufficient. One widely cited estimate: roughly 80% of "we need fine-tuning" requests are actually solved by better retrieval. Fine-tuning is for form, not facts — shaping behavior, tone, structured output format, and domain vocabulary. It is the wrong tool for injecting knowledge that changes frequently; RAG remains the right tool for that.

The three things fine-tuning genuinely fixes

Frontier models are already excellent at general instruction following. What they still consistently struggle with, and what fine-tuning is actually good for:

  • Exact output schema — a rigid, unusual structured format that needs to be followed with near-perfect consistency at scale, beyond what prompting alone reliably guarantees.
  • Narrow domain vocabulary — specialized terminology or jargon that's underrepresented in general training data.
  • Brand voice / tone — a specific, consistent style that's difficult to fully pin down through prompting alone across a large volume of generations.

The prerequisite checklist before fine-tuning makes sense

  • A stable task with a known, fixed output schema — not something still actively changing.
  • A real evaluation harness (Phase 9) that has already shown the base model plateauing with prompting and RAG alone.
  • At least a few hundred high-quality examples — reported guidance suggests 500-2,000 curated examples for instruction/tone fine-tuning, and importantly: quality over quantity. Curated examples in the low thousands commonly outperform a much larger noisy dataset.

They compose — this isn't an either/or

Fine-tuning and RAG solve different problems and stack cleanly: a fine-tuned smaller open-weight model paired with a strong RAG pipeline is cited as a strong cost-per-quality combination for many production agents in 2026 — fine-tuning shapes how the model behaves and responds, RAG supplies what it knows at any given moment.

Check your understanding

1. What's the recommended order for solving a quality problem in an LLM application?
This ordering reflects cost and reversibility — prompting is free and instant to change, while fine-tuning requires real data, compute, and evaluation investment, so it's reserved for when cheaper options are demonstrably insufficient.
2. Why is fine-tuning the wrong tool for knowledge that changes weekly?
Re-fine-tuning every time underlying facts change is far more costly and slower than retrieval, which naturally reflects updated source documents without retraining anything.
3. What does "quality over quantity" mean in the context of fine-tuning datasets?
Reported guidance specifically notes that curated examples in the hundreds to low thousands often beat tens of thousands of noisy ones — a finding consistent with broader "less but better data" results in the fine-tuning literature.
