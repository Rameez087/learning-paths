2.5 Context windows & model families

The number everyone quotes, and why bigger isn't automatically better.

TL;DR
A context window is the maximum number of tokens a model can process in one request — system prompt, conversation history, retrieved documents, and the response, all combined. Windows have grown from roughly 4K tokens (2023) to 1M+ tokens on multiple frontier models by 2026. But because attention cost grows quadratically (2.4), a bigger window costs disproportionately more to fill, and research consistently shows models get less reliable at using information buried in the middle of a very long context — a bigger window is capacity, not automatically better comprehension.

Where the 2026 landscape actually sits

As of 2026, most frontier models — Claude, GPT, Gemini — cluster in the 200K to 1M+ token range, up from roughly 4K–8K tokens as recently as 2023. A handful of models advertise even larger windows (multiple millions of tokens), but multiple independent 2026 benchmarks note a consistent gap between a model's advertised window and its effective context — the length over which retrieval quality actually holds up. Exact numbers shift model to model and month to month, so treat any specific figure as a snapshot, not a permanent fact.

Why "just use the biggest window" is bad default advice

  • Cost scales worse than linearly. Filling a 1M-token window doesn't cost roughly 8x a 128K window — it costs substantially more than that, because attention compute grows quadratically with sequence length (2.4). Several providers also apply long-context pricing surcharges specifically above certain thresholds.
  • The "lost in the middle" effect. Multiple studies on long-context retrieval find models are most reliable at using information near the start or end of a long context, and measurably less reliable at retrieving facts buried in the middle — even well within the advertised window size.
  • Latency compounds this. More tokens to attend over means slower generation, which matters directly for any user-facing application with a response-time budget.

The practical implication, which becomes central once we reach RAG in Phase 6: retrieving only the most relevant few thousand tokens for a query and feeding just those to the model usually beats dumping an entire 500-page document into a million-token window, both on cost and on answer quality.

Check your understanding

1. What does a model's context window actually include, besides your latest message?
Everything the model attends over — instructions, history, retrieved content, and its own generated output — shares the same token budget defined by the context window.
2. Why does doubling a model's context window cost more than double to fully utilize?
Because every token attends to every other token, cost grows roughly with the square of the sequence length — this is a direct consequence of how attention works, covered in 2.3–2.4.
3. What does the "lost in the middle" finding suggest about long-context usage?
This is why retrieval (RAG, Phase 6) — surfacing only the most relevant chunks instead of stuffing an entire document into context — often outperforms simply using a bigger window.
