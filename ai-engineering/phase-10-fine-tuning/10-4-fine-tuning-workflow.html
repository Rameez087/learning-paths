10.4 Fine-tuning workflow & failure modes

Fine-tuning became cheap in 2026. The failure modes did not go away just because the GPU bill got smaller.

TL;DR
A QLoRA fine-tune on a well-curated dataset can now be a genuinely fast, low-cost operation — hours, not weeks, on a single GPU. But the classic failure modes (catastrophic forgetting, overfitting on a small dataset, broken instruction-following, compute spent on the wrong problem) are exactly as real as they were when fine-tuning required a six-figure budget. The fix isn't more compute — it's evaluation discipline (Phase 9) applied specifically before and after every fine-tuning run.

Try it: diagnose the failure

The workflow, end to end

  1. Confirm the prerequisite (10.1). Stable task, known schema, a real eval showing prompting/RAG plateaued, enough curated examples.
  2. Prepare the dataset. Quality over quantity (10.1) — hundreds to low thousands of carefully curated examples, typically in a structured instruction format.
  3. Choose the method (10.2). QLoRA is the practical default for most teams in 2026 — 4-bit base, LoRA adapters, runs on accessible hardware.
  4. Train, with a held-out validation set separate from training data, to catch overfitting during the run itself rather than discovering it only after deployment.
  5. Evaluate before shipping (Phase 9). Score the fine-tuned model and the base model on identical prompts, on both task-specific metrics and general capability benchmarks — and ship only when the improvement is real and general capability hasn't regressed.

Why you benchmark general capability, not just the target task

A fine-tune can look like a clear win on its target metric while quietly degrading the model's broader capabilities — this is catastrophic forgetting showing up in aggregate. The specific practice this motivates: benchmark before and after on general capability tests (not just your narrow task metric) so a regression in unrelated capability doesn't go unnoticed simply because nobody was watching for it.

Check your understanding

1. Why does a held-out validation set matter during a fine-tuning run, not just at final evaluation?
Monitoring performance on held-out data throughout training is what lets you catch the model starting to memorize training examples rather than generalize, before that becomes a shipped problem.
2. Why benchmark general capability, not just the fine-tuning target metric, before shipping?
A narrow-metric win can mask a real regression elsewhere in the model's capabilities — checking both before and after is what catches this trade-off explicitly rather than discovering it in production.
3. Why does fine-tuning becoming cheaper in 2026 not eliminate its classic failure modes?
Lower cost changes who can afford to fine-tune and how quickly they can iterate — it doesn't change the underlying mechanics that produce forgetting or overfitting, which is why evaluation discipline remains essential regardless of budget.
