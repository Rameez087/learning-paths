12.1 Capstone: RAG system with evaluation

Everything from Phases 2, 3, 5, 6, and 9, assembled into one thing you actually built and can show.

TL;DR
Build a retrieval-augmented question-answering system over a real document set of your choosing (your own notes, a public dataset, technical documentation), with a genuine evaluation harness behind it — not just a demo that looks good on three cherry-picked queries. The evaluation is what separates this from a weekend toy project: without it, you have no way to know whether your chunking choice, your embedding model, or your prompt actually made things better or worse.

What to build

  • A document ingestion pipeline: parse, chunk (5.3), embed (5.1), and index (5.2) a real document set of at least a few hundred pages.
  • A query pipeline: embed the incoming question, retrieve top candidates, optionally rerank (6.2), assemble a prompt (Phase 3) with the retrieved context, generate an answer (Phase 4).
  • A golden dataset (9.1) of at least 30-50 real question/answer pairs against your actual document set — not generic questions, ones you'd genuinely want answered.
  • An evaluation script computing context precision, context recall, faithfulness, and answer relevancy (6.4) against that golden set.
  • At least one deliberate experiment: change chunk size, or add hybrid search (5.4), or add a reranker (6.2) — and show the eval numbers before and after, not just an assertion that it's better.

Track your progress

What "done" looks like

A README documenting: what document set you used and why, your chunking and embedding choices and why you made them, your golden dataset (or a representative sample of it), your evaluation results before and after your one deliberate experiment, and an honest paragraph on what still doesn't work well and why. That last part matters — a capstone that only shows success stories reads as incomplete to anyone technical reviewing it; showing you know exactly where the system still fails is a stronger signal than pretending it doesn't.

Common places this goes wrong

  • Skipping the golden dataset and eyeballing quality instead. Without numbers, "I improved retrieval" is just a claim, not a demonstrated result.
  • Testing on the same handful of queries you developed against. This measures whether you overfit to your own test cases, not whether the system generalizes.
  • Reporting only faithfulness, not context recall. Recall 6.4's central lesson — a generator can look faithful while the retriever is quietly missing information.

This capstone synthesizes Phases 2 (embeddings), 3 (prompting), 5 (vector search), 6 (RAG), and 9 (evaluation) into a single working project.
