12.3 Capstone: Multi-LLM orchestrated app

Phase 7, made concrete: an app that routes intelligently instead of sending every request to one model.

TL;DR
Build an application that routes requests across at least two different models based on task complexity or cost, with a fallback chain (7.2) for when a provider fails, and real observed cost/latency numbers proving the routing actually helped — not just an architecture diagram claiming it should.

What to build

  • An application with at least two genuinely different request types — e.g. simple classification/extraction versus open-ended reasoning or writing.
  • A router (7.1) sending each request type to an appropriately-sized model — a cheap, fast model for the easy majority, a stronger model reserved for the harder minority.
  • A fallback chain (7.2) that activates when the primary model for a request type fails or times out, ordered sensibly by cost (not just "whatever's available").
  • Basic caching (7.4) — even simple exact-match prompt caching counts, semantic caching if you want to go further.
  • A short benchmark: total cost and average latency with routing enabled, versus what it would have cost to send every request to your most capable model. Real numbers, not an estimate.

Track your progress

What "done" looks like

A README with your routing logic explained (and kept intentionally simple, per 7.1's specific warning against over-engineering the router), your fallback chain and why you ordered it the way you did, and a small table: cost and latency with your routing setup versus the single-model baseline. This last part is the whole point of the capstone — it's the concrete evidence that orchestration decisions produce measurable outcomes, not just architectural elegance.

Common places this goes wrong

  • A router too clever for the task. Simple rules (task type, a lightweight classifier) usually beat an elaborate AI-based routing decision — this was 7.1's most specific warning.
  • No real before/after numbers. "This should save money" is a hypothesis; a measured comparison is a result.
  • A fallback chain ordered only by preference, not cost. Recall 7.2 — an outage that fallback-jumps straight to your most expensive model quietly triples your cost on top of the disruption itself.
