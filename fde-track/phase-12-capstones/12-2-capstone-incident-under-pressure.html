12.2 Capstone: incident under pressure

It's the morning of the go-live demo for Meridian's leadership team. The support agent you built starts issuing refunds it shouldn't.

The situation: Thirty minutes before the scheduled demo to Meridian's COO and board, a monitoring alert fires: the customer support agent (from Phase 8.3's design) has issued twelve refunds above the coded $50 cap in the last ten minutes. The refund tool's hard limit should have prevented this. The demo is still scheduled to start on time.
TL;DR
This is 2.4's debugging-under-pressure discipline, at maximum stakes. Apply the method: separate containment from root cause, check what changed recently, narrate your hypothesis out loud, and decide honestly whether the demo should proceed, be delayed, or be modified — a decision Phase 9's stakeholder communication skills need to carry, live, in real time.

Work through the incident

The decision this scenario is really testing

The technical bug is one part of this. The harder, more distinctly FDE-shaped decision: what do you tell the COO thirty minutes before the demo? Recall 9.2's discipline on delivering bad news early and clearly, with a plan attached. A defensible response states plainly what happened, what's already been contained, and offers a real choice — proceed with the demo on a version you can vouch for, or delay briefly for a fix — rather than either hiding the issue or panicking visibly in front of leadership.

What "genuinely resolved" requires here

Beyond containing the immediate issue (rolling back or disabling the refund tool), a complete response needs the actual root cause identified — was the $50 cap check bypassed by a code path that skipped it, a race condition under concurrent requests, or a configuration that silently reverted during a recent deploy? Recall Phase 5.4's diagnostic discipline: confirming the fix by watching the relevant behavior return to normal, not just assuming a rollback worked, and writing the postmortem honestly once resolved (5.3).

This capstone applies debugging under pressure (2.4), the agent design from 8.3, stakeholder communication (9.2), and the diagnostic and postmortem discipline from Phase 5.
