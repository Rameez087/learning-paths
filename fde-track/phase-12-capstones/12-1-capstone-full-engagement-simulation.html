12.1 Capstone: full engagement simulation

One fictional customer. Every phase of this track, applied in sequence. No solution given upfront — you behave like an actual FDE.

The customer: Meridian Freight, a regional logistics company with 40 years of history. They run a 15-year-old on-premise warehouse management system, a newer cloud-based dispatch platform (acquired via a 2023 acquisition, still not fully integrated), and a customer-facing tracking portal built by a contractor who's no longer reachable. Leadership wants "an AI system to help reduce shipping delays." The COO, the head of warehouse operations, and the customer support lead each have a different idea of what's actually causing delays. Data privacy requirements mean customer shipping addresses can't leave their private cloud environment. Budget is fixed. Go-live is expected within one quarter.
TL;DR
Run the full lifecycle from Phase 1.3 against this scenario: Discover → Understand → Scope → Design → Prototype → Integrate → Build → Deploy → Observe → Iterate → Prove Impact → Scale → Handoff. This capstone deliberately doesn't hand you the answer — the checklist below tracks your progress through genuinely doing the work, not through reading a solution.

Work through the engagement

What genuine completion looks like at each stage

  • Discovery — you've identified that three stakeholders have three different theories about the delay's cause, and you have a plan to reconcile or test them (9.1).
  • Scope — you've turned "reduce shipping delays" into a specific, measurable target, with an explicit statement of what's out of scope for this quarter (9.3).
  • Design — your architecture accounts for the private-cloud data residency constraint (8.4's pattern), the two disconnected systems (6.1, 6.3), and states its tradeoffs plainly for both the COO and the warehouse operations lead (8.1, 9.2).
  • Integration — you've investigated both the legacy warehouse system and the dispatch platform's actual APIs (6.2, 6.3) rather than assuming a clean integration path.
  • Deployment & observability — you have a rollback plan and real monitoring in place before go-live (5.1, 5.2).
  • Prove impact — you have a specific, defensible ROI story ready for the COO, not just "the system works" (10.1).
  • Handoff — documentation and a runbook exist for whoever operates this after you (1.3, 9.2).

The honest self-assessment question

Before considering this capstone complete, ask: could you defend every design decision above to a skeptical stakeholder in the room, in plain language, with the actual tradeoff stated honestly? If any answer is "I'd need to think about that," that's a genuine signal for which phase of this track to revisit — this capstone is diagnostic as much as it is a deliverable.

This capstone synthesizes the full 12-phase track — the engagement lifecycle (1.3), enterprise integration (Phase 6), system design method (8.1), consulting skills (Phase 9), and business impact (10.1) applied together against one scenario.
