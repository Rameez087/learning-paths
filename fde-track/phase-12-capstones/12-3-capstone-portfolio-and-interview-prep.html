12.3 Capstone: portfolio & interview prep

A portfolio that proves outcomes, not tutorials — and interview practice for the specific formats FDE roles actually use.

TL;DR
An FDE portfolio needs to demonstrate the same breadth this track covers: a real integration (not a toy API call), a system design writeup showing tradeoffs stated plainly, and evidence of customer-facing judgment — even if simulated, like this track's own scenarios. FDE interviews typically span technical depth, system design under real constraints, and a case study or role-play testing discovery and communication skills together — not just a coding round.

What makes a portfolio project "prove outcomes, not tutorials"

A tutorial-following project (the fortieth to-do app, a copy-pasted RAG chatbot with no customization) demonstrates that instructions can be followed. A genuinely FDE-relevant project demonstrates judgment: a real integration with a genuinely messy API (6.3's investigation skills, applied to something real), a design writeup showing an actual tradeoff you personally weighed (8.1), or — most powerfully — a written response to one of this track's own capstone scenarios, showing the actual reasoning, not just a final answer.

The behavioral question this role specifically tests for

Beyond standard behavioral questions, FDE interviews specifically probe for comfort with ambiguity and travel, and genuine customer-facing composure under pressure — closely related to this track's 12.2 capstone. A strong answer to "tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to a stakeholder" draws on real, specific experience (even a smaller-scale one) rather than a hypothetical, and shows the same honest, plan-attached communication discipline from 9.2.
