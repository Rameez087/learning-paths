10.1 ROI & business impact

"If you build your business case around the direct inference cost line, you are wrong by a factor of four." Most of the real cost — and most of the real value — lives elsewhere.

TL;DR
ROI = (Net Gain − Total Investment) / Total Investment × 100. The trap most teams fall into: calculating ROI from initial cost only, ignoring infrastructure, maintenance, talent, and time-to-market. In real 2026 enterprise GenAI deployments, direct inference cost is typically only 20-25% of total cost — data and integration work together exceed 40%. This directly connects to Phase 1.3's "Prove Impact" stage: an FDE who can only point to "the model works" hasn't yet made the actual business case a renewal decision depends on.

Why "we saved $X in labor" is necessary but not sufficient

A genuinely complete ROI model includes labor savings, but also revenue lift, risk reduction, and the full cost side: infrastructure, data pipeline maintenance, ongoing model evaluation (Phase 7.3), and talent. Real 2026 data shows the direct model API cost is often only a quarter of total spend — the data and integration work (much of it exactly the kind of work covered in Phases 3 and 6) is frequently the larger, less visible cost, and skipping it in the business case is a documented, common cause of AI projects being abandoned after launch.

A number that survives audit

A specific, practical bar: "your CFO needs a number that survives audit." This means the ROI model needs to hold up when someone in finance actually checks it line by line — conservative assumptions stated explicitly, both direct and indirect costs included, and a benefits model that can genuinely be re-measured after launch (connecting to Phase 7.3's continuous evaluation principle) rather than a one-time optimistic projection nobody checks again.

Why the FDE specifically needs to own this number, not just the model

Recall Phase 1.4: the 2026 market has shifted toward paying a premium for engineers who can demonstrate a direct link between their deployments and customer retention. A technically excellent system with no clear, defensible ROI story is a much harder renewal conversation than a good-enough system with a rock-solid, board-presentable business case — which is exactly why this skill sits inside the FDE role rather than being purely a sales or finance function.
