10.3 Domain landscapes across industries

The same RAG architecture means something completely different when the documents are legal contracts versus patient records versus factory sensor logs.

TL;DR
An FDE doesn't need deep domain expertise in every industry — but walking into a discovery call with zero vocabulary for the customer's world wastes their time and yours. This topic is a reference, not a mastery requirement: enough shared language to ask informed questions in discovery (9.1), and enough awareness of each industry's typical constraints to avoid an obviously naive first design.

Why this reference matters more now, specifically

Recall Phase 1.4: Anthropic's $1.5B joint venture with Blackstone and Goldman Sachs is specifically targeting financial services. OpenAI's "The Deployment Company" targets Fortune 500 enterprise broadly. As frontier labs build industry-specific FDE business units, walking into a specific vertical with zero grounding in its typical systems and regulatory landscape is an increasingly real gap, not a hypothetical one.

The pattern that repeats across every industry

Despite genuinely different vocabularies and regulations, the same underlying question structure applies everywhere: what systems already hold this industry's core data (a CRM in retail, an EHR in healthcare, a core banking system in finance), what regulatory framework constrains how that data can be used (HIPAA, SOX, ITAR), and what's the actual cost of an error in this specific domain (a wrong product recommendation vs. a wrong clinical suggestion). Learning to ask these three questions for an unfamiliar industry is more durable than memorizing any one industry's specifics.

Building genuine depth in one industry over time

Most FDEs end up with real depth in one or two industries after enough engagements, not broad shallow knowledge everywhere. This is a natural, healthy specialization — the reference table above is meant to get someone started in an unfamiliar vertical quickly, not to substitute for the genuine depth that comes from actually working several engagements in a given industry.
