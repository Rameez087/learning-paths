10.2 Build vs. buy & TCO

"The enterprise AI agent market presents a genuine build-vs-buy decision for the first time — the answer changes at approximately 1 million conversations per year."

TL;DR
Buying wins on short-term ROI — faster to live, lower upfront cost, returns within months. Building wins over a 3-5 year horizon specifically when the capability is central to the business — custom systems compound in value on proprietary data, while vendor pricing compounds in cost as usage scales. A concrete 2026 data point for AI agents specifically: buy wins below roughly 1 million conversations per year; build wins above it, as per-conversation vendor pricing costs more than owning the infrastructure at real scale.

Why Year-1 cost comparisons are structurally misleading

"Every enterprise AI TCO analysis is built on the same structural error: Year 1 costs are compared, not 3-year costs." Building looks expensive in Year 1 because engineering time is genuinely being spent up front. Buying looks cheap in Year 1 because the license fee is the only visible number. By Year 3, this frequently reverses — build costs decline as the system matures, while buy costs often rise as usage scales and vendor pricing compounds.

The questions that actually determine the right answer

  • Is this capability central to the business, or commodity? A hospital's uniquely-shaped triage workflow may deserve a custom build; standard document processing a vendor already does well usually doesn't.
  • Who controls the model, and what happens to your data? A specific, pointed due-diligence question for any "buy" decision — can you export cleanly if you switch vendors later?
  • What's the real 3-year TCO, not the Year-1 number? Request vendor pricing at your actual projected production volume, not the pricing at a pilot's small scale — a vendor declining to share production-volume pricing before signature is itself a meaningful finding.

The hybrid path: increasingly the realistic answer in 2026

A specifically named, increasingly common pattern for 2026: build the differentiating "intelligence layer" — the piece trained on proprietary data that actually becomes a competitive advantage — while buying the commodity infrastructure underneath it. This fits when the use case genuinely needs both proprietary differentiation and commodity capability, and a pure build path would take too long to meet real business timelines.
