1.2 FDE vs. adjacent roles

Same rough skill set, wildly different jobs — and the useful question is always "who owns the deployment once it stops being a proof point and becomes real operational work?"

TL;DR
Five roles get confused with FDE constantly, and the confusion is understandable — all involve technical depth and customer interaction. The reliable differentiators are three specific things: ownership (what do they ultimately own — a design, a deal, or a running system?), lifecycle timing (pre-sale, at signing, or post-sale?), and coding intensity (prototype-grade or production-grade, and how much of their time is actually spent writing code?).

The lifecycle diagram that resolves most confusion

Plotting each role against the customer journey — pre-sale, at signing, rollout, ongoing operation — makes the boundaries far clearer than any list of responsibilities. A Solutions Architect is often involved before the contract is fully signed, validating technical fit and shaping the design. A Sales/Solutions Engineer lives almost entirely in the pre-sales cycle, running demos to help close deals — their primary metric is revenue influenced, not code shipped. The FDE shows up once the deal is done, to make the product actually deliver value, and stays until the customer is succeeding.

Why the confusion is genuinely reasonable

Current job markets reinforce it directly: as of mid-2026, OpenAI's careers page lists both Forward Deployed Engineer roles and a separate "Solutions Architect, Digital Natives" role. Anthropic runs an active "Forward Deployed Engineer, Applied AI" posting alongside separate Applied AI Architect roles. The titles genuinely overlap in language even at the companies that use them most precisely — which is exactly why ownership and lifecycle timing, not job titles, are the reliable signal.

vs. DevOps/SRE and AI/ML Engineer specifically

DevOps/SRE roles typically own reliability and operations for infrastructure the company already controls internally — not a specific external customer's environment with its own constraints and stakeholders. An AI/ML Engineer typically focuses on model training, fine-tuning, and evaluation as their core craft; an FDE uses those skills as one tool among many (alongside enterprise integration, cloud infrastructure, and customer communication) in service of a specific deployment outcome — the depth is narrower per skill, but the breadth across skills is much wider.
