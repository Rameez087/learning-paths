6.5 Modern warehouse architecture

The layer-cake pattern that took 5.1's "keep raw data around" principle and turned it into a full architecture.

TL;DR
The medallion architecture (bronze / silver / gold) is the dominant 2026 pattern for organizing data inside a modern warehouse or lakehouse. Bronze holds raw, untransformed data exactly as ingested. Silver holds cleaned, validated, deduplicated data — still fairly granular. Gold holds business-ready, dimensionally-modeled tables (the star schemas from 6.2) that analysts and dashboards actually query. This is ELT's "T" step (5.1), made explicit and staged rather than happening in one opaque jump from raw to final.

Why staging the transformation matters

Going straight from raw to fully-modeled in one step means any bug in that single large transformation is hard to isolate — was it a data quality issue, or a modeling error? Splitting the work into bronze → silver → gold means each stage has a narrower, more testable job: silver's job is purely "is this data clean and valid," gold's job is purely "is this correctly modeled for the business question." This mirrors 3.5's prompt-chaining principle from the AI track directly — narrow, inspectable stages beat one large opaque transformation.

Lakehouse: warehouse features on top of lake-cheap storage

A data lakehouse combines a data lake's cheap, flexible object storage (holding any file format, structured or not) with a warehouse's reliability features — transactions, schema enforcement, time travel — layered on top via open table formats like Delta Lake or Apache Iceberg. All major 2026 cloud platforms have converged on reading and writing these open formats, meaning the underlying file layer is increasingly portable across Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, and Microsoft Fabric, even as the platforms themselves compete on the compute and tooling layered on top.

Governance is a layer, not an afterthought

Modern warehouse platforms increasingly bundle governance directly into the architecture — lineage tracking, access controls, and cataloging tools (like Microsoft Purview on Azure) integrated at the platform level rather than bolted on separately. This connects forward to Phase 11's data governance topic, but it's worth flagging here: governance decisions (who can see what, how changes are tracked) are increasingly architectural decisions made when the warehouse itself is designed, not just policy documents written afterward.

Check your understanding

1. What is the job of the silver layer in medallion architecture?
Silver sits between raw (bronze) and business-ready (gold) — its narrow job is data quality, not final business modeling, which is exactly what makes each layer independently testable.
2. Why does staging the bronze-silver-gold transformation help with debugging?
This mirrors the prompt-chaining principle from the AI track — decomposing one large opaque step into narrow, inspectable stages makes it much easier to locate exactly where something went wrong.
3. What does a data lakehouse combine that makes it distinct from a traditional lake or warehouse alone?
The lakehouse concept specifically bridges the gap between a lake's storage flexibility and a warehouse's data-management guarantees, using open formats that multiple platforms can now read and write interchangeably.
