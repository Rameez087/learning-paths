6.1 OLTP vs. OLAP

The database powering your checkout button and the database powering your revenue dashboard should never be designed the same way.

TL;DR
OLTP (Online Transaction Processing) systems handle huge numbers of small, fast writes — inserts, updates, deletes — normalized (typically to 3NF) to keep data consistent and avoid duplication. OLAP (Online Analytical Processing) systems are built for reading and aggregating large historical datasets, deliberately denormalized into a dimensional model for fast, complex queries. Moving data from an OLTP application database into an OLAP warehouse means intentionally breaking normalization rules — this isn't sloppiness, it's a deliberate trade-off optimizing for a completely different access pattern.

Why denormalizing for OLAP is the right call, not a shortcut

A normalized OLTP schema minimizes redundancy by splitting data across many small, linked tables — great for fast, safe writes, but it means an analytical question ("total revenue by region last quarter") requires joining many tables together, which gets slow at the scale a warehouse operates at. A denormalized OLAP schema deliberately duplicates some data across fewer, wider tables specifically to minimize the number of joins a typical analytical query needs — trading storage efficiency and write-safety (which OLAP doesn't need much of) for read speed (which is the entire point of OLAP).

Same business, two different models

Take a ride-share app. The OLTP model, triggered every time a rider requests a ride, needs normalized tables for riders, drivers, and rides that support fast, safe concurrent writes as rides are created and updated in real time. The OLAP model of the exact same business process, built later for analytics, reorganizes this into a fact table of ride events (the measurable numbers: fare, distance, duration) surrounded by dimension tables (rider, driver, location, time) — optimized entirely for "how many rides, grouped by which attributes, over what time period" style questions.

This is exactly what ELT's "T" does

This connects directly back to 5.1: the transformation step in an ELT pipeline is very often precisely this OLTP-to-OLAP reshaping — taking normalized, transaction-shaped raw data and remodeling it into the denormalized, analysis-shaped tables covered in the rest of this phase.

Check your understanding

1. Why are OLTP systems typically normalized while OLAP systems are typically denormalized?
Each design choice matches its system's actual access pattern — OLTP's frequent small writes benefit from normalization's consistency guarantees, while OLAP's large aggregate reads benefit from denormalization's reduced join burden.
2. In the ride-share example, what does the OLAP fact table actually store?
Fact tables hold the quantitative measurements; the contextual details (who, where, when) live in the surrounding dimension tables — this split is the foundation of dimensional modeling, covered fully in 6.2.
3. How does the OLTP-to-OLAP transformation relate to the ELT pipeline covered in 5.1?
This ties two phases together directly — the dimensional modeling techniques in this phase are frequently the concrete content of the transformation logic an ELT pipeline runs.
