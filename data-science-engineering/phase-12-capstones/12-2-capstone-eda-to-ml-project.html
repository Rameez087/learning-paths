12.2 Capstone: EDA-to-ML project

Phases 3, 4, 9, and 10 — the full arc from raw numbers to a properly evaluated model.

TL;DR
Take a real dataset through the complete analytical arc: statistics and EDA first (Phases 3-4) to genuinely understand what you're working with, then feature engineering and a properly chosen model (Phase 9), evaluated with the rigor Phase 10 actually demands — a real train/validation/test split, cross-validation, and metrics chosen to fit the problem, not just accuracy by default. The evaluation discipline is the actual point of this capstone, not the model choice.

What to build

  • A real, messy-enough dataset (not a pre-cleaned toy dataset) — a public Kaggle dataset, an open government dataset, or your own collected data.
  • A genuine EDA pass (4.1): compute descriptive statistics (3.1), check distributions (3.2), and produce at least 3 visualizations that actually inform a modeling decision — not just decorative charts.
  • Deliberate feature engineering (9.3) with at least one derived feature you created from domain reasoning, not just raw columns fed in directly.
  • A model trained with a correct train/validation/test split (10.1) and k-fold cross-validation (10.2) — report the variance across folds, not just the mean.
  • At least 3 evaluation metrics appropriate to your specific problem (10.3) — if your data is imbalanced, this must include precision, recall, and F1, not just accuracy.
  • A bias-variance diagnosis (9.4): is your final model underfitting or overfitting, and what's your evidence?

Track your progress

What "done" looks like

A notebook or report with a clear narrative: what the EDA revealed and how it shaped your feature engineering, why you chose your specific model family (9.2), your cross-validation results including per-fold variance, your chosen metrics and why they fit this specific problem, and an honest bias-variance diagnosis with the actual evidence (learning curves, train vs. validation gap) that led you to that conclusion.

Common places this goes wrong

  • Skipping EDA and jumping straight to modeling. Recall 4.1's central warning — this is exactly the failure mode that produces models built on data nobody actually understood.
  • Reporting only accuracy on an imbalanced dataset. This is 10.3's accuracy paradox, made real — a lazy majority-class model would pass this bar without you noticing.
  • A single train/test split with no cross-validation. One split can get lucky; 10.2 exists specifically to catch this.

This capstone synthesizes Phases 3 (statistics), 4 (EDA & visualization), 9 (ML fundamentals), and 10 (evaluation) into a single working project.
