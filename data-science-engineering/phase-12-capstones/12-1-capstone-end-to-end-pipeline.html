12.1 Capstone: End-to-end data pipeline

Phases 1, 5, 6, 7, and 11 — assembled into one real, working pipeline you built yourself.

TL;DR
Build a real ELT pipeline: extract data from a genuine source (a public API, a CSV export, a small database), land it raw, transform it through bronze/silver/gold layers (6.5) into a proper star schema (6.2), orchestrate it with Airflow or Dagster (Phase 7) on a schedule, and add at least one data quality test (11.2) that would actually catch a realistic failure. This capstone is deliberately about the full chain working together, not any single piece in isolation.

What to build

  • A real data source — a public API (weather, sports stats, a government open-data portal), or a CSV/Excel export from somewhere you actually care about.
  • An extraction step that lands raw data untransformed (5.1's ELT principle) into a local database or a free-tier cloud warehouse.
  • A transformation layer organized as bronze → silver → gold (6.5), culminating in at least one proper star schema with a fact table and 2+ dimension tables (6.2).
  • Orchestration via Airflow or Dagster (7.1-7.2) running on an actual schedule, with retries configured (7.3) for at least one plausibly flaky step.
  • At least 2 dbt-style tests (11.2) — not_null, unique, or a custom range check — that would genuinely catch a realistic data quality failure in your specific pipeline.

Track your progress

What "done" looks like

A README documenting your source choice and why, your bronze/silver/gold transformation logic, your star schema design (which columns are facts, which are dimensions, and why), your orchestration schedule and retry configuration, and — importantly — what your data quality tests actually check and why you picked those specific checks over other possible ones.

Common places this goes wrong

  • Skipping the raw/bronze layer entirely — jumping straight from source to final model loses 5.1's core benefit: the ability to re-derive a corrected transformation without re-extracting.
  • A "star schema" that's really just one flat denormalized table — recall 6.2's actual test: does this column belong in a fact table (aggregated) or dimension table (filtered/grouped)?
  • Tests that would never actually fail on realistic bad data — a not_null test on a column that's never actually null in practice doesn't demonstrate you understood 11.2's purpose.

This capstone synthesizes Phases 1 (foundations), 5 (data engineering fundamentals), 6 (warehousing), 7 (orchestration), and 11 (data quality) into a single working project.
