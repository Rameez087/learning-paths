5.1 ETL vs. ELT

Revisiting Phase 1.2's preview — now with the full architectural picture.

TL;DR
ETL (Extract, Transform, Load) transforms data in a separate processing step before it ever reaches the warehouse. ELT (Extract, Load, Transform) loads raw data first, then transforms it inside the warehouse using its own compute. ELT became the 2026 default for most analytical workloads because cloud warehouses got powerful and cheap enough to handle transformation directly, and keeping raw data around turned out to be genuinely valuable — it means any future transformation need can be met without re-extracting from the original source.

Why raw data retention is the real unlock

In ETL, once data is transformed and loaded, the original raw form is often discarded or hard to re-derive — if a bug is later found in the transformation logic, or a new team needs the data shaped differently, you may need to re-extract from the source system entirely, which can be slow, costly, or sometimes impossible if the source has since changed. In ELT, the raw layer persists indefinitely, so a bug fix or a new use case is just a new transformation query against data that's already sitting there.

When ETL still makes sense

ELT being the default doesn't mean ETL is obsolete. ETL remains the right choice when transformation must happen before data lands anywhere for compliance reasons (e.g. certain PII must be masked or removed before it's stored at all, not after), or when the destination system genuinely can't handle the compute load of in-place transformation.

Where this connects to what's next in this phase

The "L" (load) step in ELT is where 5.2's batch-vs-streaming decision and 5.4's ingestion strategy choices actually live. The "T" (transform) step, once data is loaded, is where dbt (from Phase 1.3) and the medallion architecture (5.3) do their work — raw data becomes progressively cleaner through defined stages, entirely within the warehouse.

Check your understanding

1. What is the core sequencing difference between ETL and ELT?
The letters spell out the actual order of operations — this reordering is the entire distinction, and it's what determines whether raw data persists in the warehouse or not.
2. Why does retaining raw data in ELT provide real practical value, not just architectural elegance?
Re-extraction from a source system can be slow, costly, or sometimes impossible if the source has changed — having the raw data already present removes that entire risk.
3. When might ETL still be the right choice over ELT?
These are genuine, specific exceptions — compliance requirements and destination compute limitations — not a general size-based rule.
